# Meeting Transcript — "Impact of Social Media on the Youth of Abu Dhabi"

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Duration:** 10:00 – 10:45 (45 minutes)
**Participants:**
- **Noora (Moderator / Methodology Facilitator)**
- **Aisha (Statistician)**
- **Mohammed (Social Policy Specialist)**
- **Fatima (Youth Affairs)**
- **Ahmed (Data & IT)**

> The discussion is a mix of Arabic and English, as spoken.

---

### 10:00 — Opening and objective

**Noora:** صباح الخير جميعاً. Let's start. Today's topic is "Impact of Social Media on the Youth of Abu Dhabi". We need to agree on a clear statistical objective before anything else.

**Mohammed:** I think the goal is simple — نحن نريد أن نفهم كيف تؤثر وسائل التواصل الاجتماعي على الشباب. But "impact" is too vague. We need to be specific.

**Aisha:** Agreed. For a proper indicator, the objective should be measurable. I propose: "قياس أثر استخدام وسائل التواصل الاجتماعي على رفاهية وسلوك الشباب في إمارة أبوظبي".

**Noora:** Good. So the objective is to measure the impact of social media use on the well-being and behaviour of youth in Abu Dhabi emirate. Does everyone agree?

**Fatima:** Yes, but I want to include education outcomes too — school performance and concentration.

**Aisha:** Then we can phrase it as well-being, behaviour, and educational outcomes. That covers the mental, social, and academic dimensions.

**Noora:** Let's lock that in: the statistical objective is to measure the impact of social media usage on the well-being, behaviour, and educational outcomes of youth in Abu Dhabi.

---

### 10:07 — Defining "youth"

**Noora:** Next, the target population. Who exactly is "youth"?

**Mohammed:** The UAE Youth Strategy defines youth as ages 15 to 35. لكن هذا النطاق واسع جداً.

**Aisha:** For statistical purposes, many international bodies use 15 to 24. UNESCO uses 15–24. Some use 15–29.

**Fatima:** Our youth programmes cover 15 to 29. School and early career. Above 29 is a different life stage.

**Ahmed:** Practically, social media behaviour is very different between a 15-year-old and a 30-year-old. Maybe we should disaggregate by age group anyway.

**Noora:** Let's set the target population as individuals aged 15 to 29, and we will disaggregate by age group. Agreed?

**Aisha:** Agreed — 15 to 29, both Emirati and non-Emirati residents? أم فقط المواطنين؟

**Mohammed:** For a national indicator, I think we should cover everyone residing in Abu Dhabi, but separate Emirati and non-Emirati in the breakdown.

**Noora:** So target population: all youth aged 15–29 residing in Abu Dhabi emirate, with nationality as a breakdown dimension.

---

### 10:14 — Statistical unit and reference period

**Noora:** Statistical unit and reference period.

**Aisha:** The statistical unit is the individual — a single young person. That's clear.

**Mohammed:** Correct, الفرد — we are measuring individuals, not households.

**Noora:** Reference period — what time window are we measuring?

**Fatima:** Social media trends change fast. I would use the most recent 12 months.

**Aisha:** If the indicator is annual, the reference period should be the latest calendar year. Let's use 2025.

**Ahmed:** But we can also collect data for a specific reference date for point-in-time measures, like "average daily screen time in the past week".

**Noora:** Let's set the reference period as calendar year 2025, and for time-use questions we use "the past 7 days" as the recall period.

---

### 10:21 — Frequency and geographic scope

**Noora:** How often do we produce this indicator, and what geography?

**Mohammed:** أعتقد سنوي. An annual indicator makes sense for policy. Quarterly is too much work.

**Fatima:** I agree annual, but we should do an annual update with a mid-year monitoring of trends.

**Noora:** Frequency: annual. Geographic scope?

**Aisha:** Abu Dhabi emirate, covering the three regions — Abu Dhabi city, Al Ain, and Al Dhafra.

**Noora:** So geographic scope: Abu Dhabi emirate, all three regions, with region as a breakdown dimension.

---

### 10:27 — Definitions: "social media" and "impact"

**Noora:** We need clear definitions, especially "social media".

**Ahmed:** Social media means platforms like Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, X (Twitter), YouTube, WhatsApp, and Facebook. We should define it as "digital platforms that enable user-generated content and social interaction".

**Fatima:** And "impact" — we said well-being, behaviour, and educational outcomes. We need indicators for each.

**Aisha:** For well-being: a mental health score, self-reported stress or anxiety. For behaviour: hours of daily usage, sleep impact. For education: self-reported concentration, school grades.

**Mohammed:** Let's also capture the positive side — not everything is negative. Social media can support learning and connectivity.

**Noora:** Important point. The indicator should measure both positive and negative impact, not assume harm.

---

### 10:34 — Data sources

**Noora:** Where does the data come from?

**Aisha:** A dedicated youth survey is the core — we can run it through SCAD. Sample youth aged 15–29 across the three regions.

**Ahmed:** We can also use administrative data — education records for school outcomes, and platform usage analytics if accessible.

**Fatima:** There are existing youth surveys and the annual well-being survey. We should reuse those where possible.

**Noora:** Data sources: (1) SCAD youth survey (primary), (2) education administrative records, (3) existing well-being survey. Agreed?

**Aisha:** Agreed. The survey is the primary source.

---

### 10:39 — Indicators and dimensions

**Noora:** Let's finalise the key indicators and dimensions.

**Aisha:** Indicators:
1. Average daily social media usage (hours).
2. Percentage of youth reporting high social media use (3+ hours/day).
3. Percentage reporting negative impact on mental well-being.
4. Percentage reporting negative impact on sleep.
5. Percentage reporting impact on educational performance.

**Noora:** Dimensions for breakdown?

**Mohammed:** Age group, gender, nationality (Emirati/non-Emirati), region, and education level.

**Aisha:** Yes, those five dimensions. And maybe employment status.

**Noora:** Let's include: age group, gender, nationality, region, education level, and employment status.

---

### 10:42 — Wrap-up and open questions

**Noora:** Summary of what we agreed:
- Objective: measure impact of social media on well-being, behaviour, and educational outcomes of youth.
- Population: youth aged 15–29 residing in Abu Dhabi.
- Unit: individual.
- Reference period: 2025 (7-day recall for time use).
- Frequency: annual.
- Scope: Abu Dhabi emirate (three regions).
- Sources: SCAD youth survey (primary), education records, well-being survey.

**Fatima:** One open question — should we define "high usage" as 3 hours or 4 hours? We said 3+ hours, but let's confirm with research.

**Ahmed:** Also, the platform list — do we include emerging platforms? This needs a decision.

**Mohammed:** وأيضاً، هل نقيس التأثير على المدى القصير فقط أم نتابع الشباب مع الوقت؟ A longitudinal follow-up is worth considering.

**Noora:** Good. We have three open questions: the "high usage" threshold, the final platform list, and whether to add a longitudinal follow-up. We'll resolve these next session.

**Aisha:** شكراً — this was a productive session. The methodology is almost complete.

**Noora:** Thank you all. We'll circulate the draft methodology for review.

---

*End of meeting — 10:45*
